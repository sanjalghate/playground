Duplicate a test suite — how should the dialog show schedules?

LCNC-18743 (Copy test suite) × LCNC-17530 (Multiple schedules, in Testing, Design + Product GTG 17 Aug) · seven options · apps/lcnc-webapp/src/features/TestSuites/
This is why the earlier design needs revisiting. The Copy Test Suite PRD (FR-5) assumes one schedule and prescribes a single line — “Schedule: Weekdays, 09:00 IST”. Multiple Schedules is already in Testing with product and design sign-off, so a suite will carry N schedules of two different kinds. A one-schedule sentence is wrong by construction, and the interesting design question is no longer whether to mention the schedule but how to show a list of them without turning a create dialog into a settings screen.
What is fixed, and what is being decided
Fixed — not up for review Name field, pre-filled Copy of <name> and editable, with the existing per-project uniqueness check · one line stating tests are shared, not copied · no “what carries over” block (removed on your instruction) · no dropped-configuration notice (FR-7 resolved as carry everything)
Being decidedThe schedule area only. Everything above and below it is identical across all seven options, so the comparison is clean.
Constraint from the briefA carried schedule must not run until the author enables it, and the dialog must say so. Testim’s cloned schedules turn themselves on and fire — the named anti-pattern.
Constraint from the KBInteraction friction caps a standard task at 4 inputs; 6+ needs redesign. Any option that asks for a decision per schedule spends inputs proportional to N.
Scale evidence18.1% of paid suites have an active schedule. One account runs nine identical suites named Taxonomy check 9 AM through 5 PM — under multiple schedules that becomes one suite with nine schedules. That is the stress case, and it is real. source: Copy Test Suite PRD [BQ 90d]
Vocabulary to reuseThe shipped Schedules modal already names rows Schedule 1…N, tags interval ones Interval, and summarises each in one line. Reuse it — do not invent a second way to describe a schedule. source: your screenshots of LCNC-17530; auto-numbering confirmed in that ticket’s design review
Schedules on the source suite: Switches every option below at once. 0 is the majority case (about four suites in five), 9 is the stress case.

Two things worth noticing. First, a schedule’s identity is only a number — Schedule 1 carries no meaning, so a list of nine tells the author very little about which to keep. Second, the row already has a natural right-hand slot (currently edit and delete icons) — which is where a per-schedule control would sit if we wanted one.
The options

Seven ways to show carried schedules

Each shows the same dialog with only the schedule area swapped. Use the switcher above to change how many schedules the source has — the differences between these options are almost entirely about what happens as that number grows.

The trade-off in one sentence each

What you give up by picking each one
S1 · Count onlyYou give up ever letting the author keep a schedule on at duplicate time, and they cannot see which schedules came across without leaving and looking.
S2 · Read-only listYou give up the decision entirely — which is defensible, but the dialog then grows with N for information the author cannot act on.
S3 · One switch for allYou give up granularity: the nine-schedule author must turn all nine on or leave all nine off, and turning all nine on is precisely the parallel-contention harm the brief warns about.
S4 · Switch per scheduleYou give up the dialog being a dialog. Nine switches is configuration, and it duplicates a surface that already exists.
S5 · Collapsed, expandableYou give up certainty that the author read it — progressive disclosure means most people never expand. Mitigated because the closed state still states the outcome.
S6 · Defer to Schedules modalYou give up single-step completion: the author lands on a copy whose schedules are all paused and must open another surface to change that.
S7 · Don’t carry, offer toYou give up the brief’s main convenience claim — never retyping cron, timezone and cadence — and you contradict two requirements to get it.
Recommendation

S5 if you want one dialog to handle every N. S3 if you want the smallest change.

Two are worth taking to review; the rest are useful mainly as the reasoning that eliminated them.

The two to discuss
S5 — collapsed,
expandable
the scaling answer		 Closed, it is one line and satisfies the brief at any N — including the 80% case where there are no schedules and the row vanishes entirely. Opened, it becomes S4 and gives the nine-schedule author exactly the granularity they need. It is the only option whose cost does not grow with N while still allowing per-schedule control. Cost: a disclosure pattern, and the honest risk that most authors never open it.
S3 — one switch
for all
the smallest change		 Closest to what the PRD’s review round already argued for, just generalised from one schedule to a list. One decision, one control, no new pattern. It breaks down only at the high end — and the high end is a handful of concentrated accounts, which may be an acceptable trade for a Secondary-importance feature.
Worth raising in the review, because they change the shape of the answer
Does the schedule
decision belong here
at all?		 S6 is the argument that it does not. The Schedules modal already exists, already handles N, both kinds, add, edit and delete — and the copy is going to be edited anyway, since 90% of same-membership suite pairs differ in at least one setting. If product accepts a two-step flow, S6 is the least code and the least new design of any option here.
Is “Schedule 3”
enough to decide on?		 Schedules are identified only by number. At N=9 the author is asked which of Schedule 1…9 to keep on, with only a cadence line to tell them apart. Any option offering per-schedule control (S4, S5) is offering a choice the labels may not support. This is an argument for nameable schedules — which belongs to LCNC-17530, not here.
Interval schedules
are the sharper risk		 A day-based schedule fires once; Every 2 hours from 02:00 fires twelve times a day. If a copied interval schedule came across enabled, it would contend for parallels twelve times daily rather than once. Whatever we pick, the paused default matters more for interval schedules — and none of the options currently distinguish the two kinds beyond reusing the Interval tag.
Sequencing between
the two tickets		 Multiple Schedules is in Testing; copy-suite has not started. If it ships first, the dialog must be built list-first — every option here assumes that. If the order slips, S1 and S6 are the only two that read correctly against a single schedule and a list, because neither renders per-schedule rows.
